# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Project management processes

OctoAcme manages projects through a consistent lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective closeout. Initiation validates the business need, stakeholders, measurable success criteria, timeline, and resource needs in a Project One-pager before approved work moves into planning. Planning turns the initiative into a prioritized, estimated backlog with clear acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, dependencies, milestones, and a release plan.

During execution, teams use project boards and iterative delivery to track work from backlog through review, QA, and completion. The Project Manager coordinates schedules, risks, resources, meetings, documentation, and status reporting, while the Product Manager defines outcomes, prioritizes the backlog, and makes value-based trade-offs. Developers implement and test functionality, participate in reviews, estimate work, and identify technical risks; QA validates acceptance criteria and quality requirements. Stakeholders provide inputs and approvals throughout the lifecycle.

Communication emphasizes transparency and prompt escalation. Delivery teams hold regular standups, the PM and Product Manager meet weekly, and milestone reviews, stakeholder updates, risk-register reviews, and a single source of project status keep dependencies and decisions visible. Blockers escalate from team-level triage to the PM, Product Lead or dependent teams, and sponsor-level escalation for significant business impact.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout delivery. Work uses unit and integration tests, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, security scanning, CI-based linting and tests, manual QA when needed, and PR approvals under the documented review policy. Pre-release checks require acceptance criteria to be met, CI and security checks to pass, prepared rollback or mitigation plans, smoke tests, and release notes. Retrospectives capture lessons and assign improvement actions with owners and due dates, with follow-up tracked for continuous improvement.
